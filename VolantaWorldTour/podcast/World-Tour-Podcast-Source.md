# Around the World from Weeze – 423 Flights, One Airbus, One Helicopter, and Absolutely No Shortcuts

*Source document for an audio episode (NotebookLM). Status: 30 September 2026.*

---

## Notes for the hosts

- **Tone:** fun, curious, a little nerdy, full of wonder. This is a travel show where the traveller happens to fly the plane themselves, in a flight simulator, on the other side of the planet, from their living room.
- **Listeners:** flight-sim fans and armchair travellers. Explain jargon in half a sentence ("ILS, the instrument landing system", "a visual approach, meaning the pilot flies by looking out of the window").
- **Names:** say airport and place names, not four-letter codes. The codes are included for reference only.
- **Refer to the creator as "the pilot" or "our pilot"** (they/them). Their home airport is Weeze, a small airport on the Lower Rhine in western Germany, near the Dutch border.
- **Don't rush the highlights.** The heart of the episode is the long list of spectacular approaches, strange islands and "wait, you can land an Airbus *there*?" moments. Use them all.

## 1. The pitch in one breath

A flight-sim pilot sits at home in Weeze. They decide to fly around the entire world. They don't want the lazy version with a few long-haul hops. They want to **land in every country and territory the flight-tracking app Volanta counts: all 245 of them.**

Their ride is a **Fenix A320**, one of the most detailed airliner simulations ever made. For the places no airliner can reach, they bring a helicopter, the **Airbus H160**. The simulator is **Microsoft Flight Simulator 2024**.

The result is a world tour that:

- has **423 flights**;
- takes about **450 hours** of flying;
- covers **127,000 nautical miles**, nearly six times around the equator;
- runs from the Arctic at 78° North to a blue-ice runway in Antarctica at almost 80° South;
- visits **403 airports**.

Then it comes home to Weeze.

This isn't just a list of flights either. It's a full interactive web app with a map, a spinning globe, flight briefings, scenery recommendations and progress sync. It was built and reviewed over several rounds with the help of AI.

## 3. The house rules: this tour has principles

The tour follows strict rules, and they make the story more interesting.

1. **Every country, no exceptions.** All 245 Volanta categories. That includes the Vatican, Monaco, San Marino, Liechtenstein, Andorra, Tuvalu, Nauru, Niue, North Korea, Yemen (via the island of Socotra), the French Southern Territories, the British Indian Ocean Territory and Antarctica.
2. **Only airports that are actually operating.**
   - No nostalgia trips to closed airports. Hong Kong's legendary **Kai Tak** (closed since 1998) is deliberately *not* on the list; the tour lands at the modern Chek Lap Kok instead.
   - Closed or dangerous airports were swapped for active alternatives:
     - Jerusalem's Atarot (closed since 2001) → the Ramallah heliport;
     - war-damaged Khartoum → Port Sudan;
     - Port-au-Prince, often shut by gang violence → Cap-Haïtien in northern Haiti.
   - The one honest exception is **Lviv in Ukraine**. It's the only way to "collect" Ukraine, but real civil airspace there has been closed since 2022. The tour marks it openly as a simulator-only stop.
3. **Keep flights short.** The target is under two hours per leg, so each evening is one or two satisfying hops, not a transatlantic snooze-fest.
4. **No Twin Otter.** Version 1 used the rugged Twin Otter bush plane for a couple of legs. Version 2 dropped it completely: everything is flown in the A320 or the H160.
5. **Honest scenery advice.** Every airport was checked:
   - Is it one of the pilot's own purchased sceneries?
   - Is it handcrafted by Microsoft/Asobo in the base simulator?
   - Is there excellent free scenery from the community?
   - Is there paid scenery worth the money?

   Freeware needs at least 4.5 stars with 8+ ratings (or 6,000+ downloads) *and* proof that it works in MSFS 2024. Paid scenery needs at least 4.3 stars from 10+ ratings. Purchases are recommended only when they add something extraordinary to the approach. Result: **zero airports left unchecked**.

## 4. The grand route: 28 chapters, one planet

The tour is organised like a travel book. Each chapter has a theme and a recommended season, so the pilot can match the real-world weather: Iceland in midsummer, Patagonia in the southern summer, the Himalaya after the monsoon.

Below is every chapter with its highlights. "★★" marks a top-tier highlight in the tour's own rating system; "★" marks a notable stop.

### Chapter 1 – North Atlantic
*Channel Islands, Faroes, Iceland and Greenland to Newfoundland · best June–August for endless daylight*

- **Weeze** – home base and the start and finish line.
- A warm-up through the pilot's own sceneries: **Amsterdam Schiphol** (FlyTampa), **Brussels**, **London Heathrow** with a westerly approach right across London.
- **Jersey** – a short 1,705 m runway on a clifftop plateau. Tight for an A320. From here the first helicopter side quest hops 21 nautical miles across to **Guernsey**, low along the cliffs.
- ★★ **Vágar, Faroe Islands** – the runway lies between mountains and the famous **Sørvágsvatn**, the "floating lake" that seems to hover above the ocean cliffs before spilling into the sea as the Bøsdalafossur waterfall. Wind and low cloud included.
- ★★ **Akureyri, Iceland** – a long approach down **Eyjafjörður, Iceland's longest fjord**, between snow-covered mountains.
- **Keflavík** – lava fields to the horizon.
- ★★ **Nuuk, Greenland** – a brand-new 2,200 m runway between fjords and skerries. Nerd drama: the default simulator still shows the old, broken layout, so the pilot must use their own purchased M'M Simulations scenery.
- Across the Arctic: **Iqaluit** on Baffin Island, **Kuujjuaq**, **Wabush** in Labrador, **Ottawa**.
- ★ **Saint-Pierre** – a tiny piece of *France* off the coast of Newfoundland.
- **St. John's** – the easternmost point of North America.

### Chapter 2 – East Coast & Caribbean
*Potomac, Bermuda and the arc of the Lesser Antilles · December–April, dry season, outside hurricane season*

- **Boston Logan** (FlyTampa) and ★ **New York JFK**, with the famous **Canarsie approach** curving over Jamaica Bay.
- ★★ **Washington National – the River Visual.** A visual approach snaking down the **Potomac River**, past Georgetown and the Pentagon, with the National Mall in view. The prohibited zone over the White House is right next to the path, so hold that line!
- ★ **Bermuda** – a runway between Castle Harbour and turquoise lagoons.
- ★★ **Sint Maarten – Maho Beach.** The most famous beach approach on Earth: over the bay, over the sunbathers, over the road, onto the runway. Three helicopter side quests start here:
  - **Anguilla**, 10 miles across the channel;
  - French **Saint-Martin** (Grand Case), its own "country" in Volanta;
  - ★ **St. Barth**, where you skim the legendary hill at the runway threshold, the Col de la Tourmente.
- Island hopping at its finest: St. Kitts with its volcano, Antigua (plus a helicopter trip to volcanic **Montserrat**, circling the exclusion zone of the Soufrière Hills), Guadeloupe, and…
- ★★ **Dominica** – a new stop! Its runway was lengthened to 1,936 m in 2025, so it's now reachable in the A320 instead of by helicopter.
- Martinique with Mont Pelée, ★ St. Lucia with the Pitons, St. Vincent, Barbados, Grenada, Tobago.
- ★ **Bonaire** – salt flats and flamingos.
- Curaçao, Aruba and on to **Caracas**.

### Chapter 3 – The Guianas & the Amazon
*Tropical coast, Amazon, Sugarloaf and Iguazú · June–November, dry season in the Amazon basin*

- Guyana, Suriname, **French Guiana** (near Kourou, Europe's spaceport).
- **Macapá** – right *on the Equator* at the mouth of the Amazon.
- ★ **Santarém** – where the Tapajós meets the Amazon.
- **Manaus** – the "meeting of the waters" of the Rio Negro and the Solimões.
- Brasília, Oscar Niemeyer's planned capital.
- ★★ **Rio de Janeiro Santos Dumont** – a city airport right under **Sugarloaf Mountain**, with a runway of just 1,323 m sticking into Guanabara Bay. Depending on the wind you fly past Sugarloaf or over the bay and the Rio–Niterói bridge. Real airlines fly A320s here with a special short-runway package.
- ★ **Foz do Iguaçu** – the waterfalls at the triple border.
- Asunción, Montevideo, Buenos Aires.

### Chapter 4 – Patagonia & Antarctica
*Lakes, glaciers, the Falklands, Tierra del Fuego and blue ice · November–January only, because Union Glacier is only open in the southern summer*

- ★ **Bariloche** – approach over Lake Nahuel Huapi with the Andes behind.
- ★★ **El Calafate** – Lago Argentino and the glaciers of the Southern Patagonian Ice Field. Expect brutal westerly crosswinds.
- ★ **The Falkland Islands** – RAF Mount Pleasant, raw and windy.
- ★★ **Ushuaia, Tierra del Fuego** – approach over the **Beagle Channel**, with gusty winds tumbling off the mountains.
- **Punta Arenas** on the Strait of Magellan – the jumping-off point for…
- ★★ **UNION GLACIER, ANTARCTICA.** The boss fight of the whole tour.
  - A **3,000 m runway made of bare blue ice** in the Ellsworth Mountains.
  - In real life the operator flies a **Boeing 757** here from Punta Arenas, about 4¼ hours.
  - Braking action: almost none. The pilot has to test landing distance and deceleration in a practice flight first.
  - The airfield isn't even in the default simulator; a free "Antarctica Airfield Pack" is mandatory.
  - The backup plan is Wilkins runway, where a real Airbus A319 has landed.
  - At 1,626 nautical miles each way, it's one of the longest legs of the tour.
- Back north through Chilean Patagonia: Balmaceda, Puerto Montt with the Osorno volcano, Concepción, **Santiago** framed by the Andes.

### Chapter 5 – Andes & Pacific Coast
*Atacama, Altiplano, Cusco, Galápagos and the Avenue of Volcanoes · May–September for dry mountains*

- The **Atacama** desert at Copiapó and Antofagasta.
- ★★ **La Paz / El Alto, Bolivia – 4,061 m.** One of the highest international airports in the world. The air is so thin that touchdown groundspeed is scary-fast and takeoff runs are huge. Mount Illimani (6,438 m) watches from the southeast.
- ★★ **Cusco, Peru – 3,310 m.** A valley approach into a narrow Andean canyon onto a sloping runway: the gateway to Machu Picchu. One of the six recommended purchases, because the FSDG scenery is the only serious version.
- Lima, Chiclayo, Guayaquil.
- ★★ **Galápagos (Baltra)** – volcanic islands in the middle of the Pacific, with free community scenery.
- ★ **Quito** – 2,390 m up on the "Avenue of the Volcanoes".
- ★ **Bogotá** at 2,550 m, Cali, colonial **Cartagena**, then **Panama City** near the canal.

### Chapter 6 – Central America & the Greater Antilles
*Volcanoes, Toncontín and the big Caribbean islands · December–April*

- San José (Costa Rica), Managua.
- ★★ **Toncontín, Tegucigalpa, Honduras – the notorious one.** A tight turn just before the threshold over hilly city streets. Big jets moved to a new airport in 2021, but Toncontín is still open, and the A320 can still do it: "Light load, stable in the turn, touch down early."
- San Salvador, **Guatemala City** with the volcanoes Agua and Fuego, **Belize** and its barrier reef.
- Cancún, Grand Cayman, Montego Bay (Jamaica).
- ★ **Cap-Haïtien** – Haiti's active gateway, with the Citadelle Laferrière fortress in the mountains.
- Puerto Plata, **Turks & Caicos**, Eleuthera, Nassau, **Havana**, Mérida, Veracruz.
- ★ **Mexico City** – 2,230 m up, landing into a sea of houses with Popocatépetl on the horizon.

### Chapter 7 – Mexico, the Rockies & Hawaii
*Baja, deserts, the Grand Tetons and the leap to Hawaii*

- Guadalajara, **Los Cabos**, Hermosillo in the Sonoran Desert.
- **Phoenix**, plus a helicopter side quest up to **Sedona** among the red rocks (with the pilot's own scenery).

- **Denver** (Flightbeam), in front of the Rockies.
- ★★ **Jackson Hole** – the only commercial airport inside a US national park, right under the **Grand Tetons**. Handcrafted in World Update 22; United flies A320s here.
- Salt Lake City by the Great Salt Lake.
- ★ **San Francisco** (Flightbeam), then **the longest flight of the tour: 2,055 nautical miles across the Pacific to Kona**, where the runway sits between black lava fields.
- ★ **Honolulu** (iniBuilds) – Diamond Head and Waikīkī.

### Chapter 8 – Micronesia
*Midway, Wake and the atolls of the Western Pacific · January–April*

- ★ **Midway Atoll** – Henderson Field, scene of the 1942 battle and home to an enormous albatross colony.
- **Wake Island** – a speck of coral in the middle of nowhere.
- **Majuro** – a runway on the thin ring of an atoll in the Marshall Islands.
- ★ **Pohnpei** – a volcanic island with a reef edge.

- Guam, Saipan, **Palau** and its Rock Islands.

### Chapter 9 – The South Seas
*Tuvalu, Samoa, the Cook Islands, Tahiti and Norfolk · May–October · the longest chapter in flying time, about 29 hours*

- Honiara on Guadalcanal.
- **Nauru** – where the runway takes up a good chunk of the island's coastline.
- **Tarawa**, Kiribati.
- ★★ **Funafuti, Tuvalu** – a runway in the middle of an atoll, lagoon on one side and ocean on the other. In real life only turboprops land here. For the A320 this is a **"special stage"** that the pilot must test first in free flight. If it works, it replaces a 10½-hour chain of helicopter hops.
- Wallis, Samoa, **Pago Pago** (an approach between steep green mountains), **Niue** (a coral rock in the South Pacific).
- **Rarotonga**, Cook Islands – lagoon and green peaks.
- ★ **Tahiti** – with Moorea across the water. From here the ★ **helicopter side quest to Bora Bora**: 140 nautical miles past Moorea and the Leeward Islands, then over the famous lagoon.
- Tonga, **Fiji**, Vanuatu, New Caledonia's UNESCO lagoon.
- ★ **Norfolk Island** – a 1,950 m runway on a cliff island.
- Auckland.

### Chapter 10 – New Zealand & Eastern Australia
*Volcanoes, the Southern Alps, the Harbour Bridge and the Whitsundays · December–March*

- Gold Coast.
- ★★ **Hamilton Island** – in the middle of the **Whitsundays** on the Great Barrier Reef. The runway ends in the sea at *both* ends, and **Whitehaven Beach** is just a few miles away.

### Chapter 15 – Karakoram & Himalaya
*Skardu, Leh, Kathmandu, Paro and the Bengal Delta · October–November, clearest mountain views after the monsoon*

- ★★ **Skardu, Pakistan** – the Indus valley in the **Karakoram**, the gateway to K2. The surrounding peaks are well over 5,000 m. Real airlines fly A320s here. A recommended purchase.

- ★★ **Kathmandu** – a valley bowl at 1,338 m with the Himalaya on the horizon and a steep descent over the southern hills. From here, the most legendary helicopter side quest of the tour: ★★ **Lukla**, below.
- ★★ **Paro, Bhutan** – *the* approach.
  - Flown visually through the narrow Paro valley at 2,245 m.
  - The aiming point for runway 15 is a house nicknamed **"Mr. Smith's House."**

### Chapter 21 – South Atlantic & the Gulf of Guinea

  - In real life an Embraer E190 serves the island; a 737-800 and an A318 have landed here.

## 6. The "special stages": where the Airbus gets nervous

Three stops are officially flagged as **special stages**. Real airlines don't fly an A320 there, so the pilot must test each one in free flight first, with a backup plan ready.

- **Union Glacier, Antarctica** – a blue-ice runway with almost no braking. Real-life ride: Boeing 757.
- **Funafuti, Tuvalu** – 1,536 m of runway in the middle of an atoll. Real-life ride: turboprops.

## 8. Breaking news from the hangar: Skiathos

Just this week (end of September 2026) the developer **M'M Simulations**, who also made the pilot's Nuuk scenery, released the **first native MSFS 2024 scenery for Skiathos**.

Skiathos is the "St. Maarten of Greece":

- a runway of just 1,628 × 30 m;
- spotters on the beach and the road at the end of the runway;
- low passes that make every holiday video go viral;
- in September 2024, even an A321 landed there.

The new scenery promises detailed terminal and tower interiors, handcrafted terrain, local landmarks and night lighting, for about €15.

So did the pilot buy it immediately? **Not yet.** The tour's own rules say that paid scenery needs at least 4.3 stars from 10+ ratings, and this one is two days old with zero reviews. On top of that, M'M's track record is mixed: one of their airports sits at 4.7 stars, another at 3.6.

So Skiathos is on the **"watch list"**. It gets promoted to a buy recommendation once the reviews come in. Discipline!

## 11. Behind the scenes: how this thing was built

This isn't a spreadsheet; it's an app. The tour lives in an interactive web page in an "instrument panel" design:

- **A world map, a spinning 3D globe and a per-flight zoom.** The map was just made zoomable with mouse, keyboard and two-finger pinch.
- **A leg bar across the top** showing the whole journey, with progress.
- **A briefing for every flight:** distance, estimated time, the highlight, tips like "light load, early stabilised", and the scenery recommendation with links.
- **Live tracking of which Volanta countries are already collected.**
- **Progress sync across devices**, so the tablet next to the simulator and the PC stay in step. There's also a debriefing archive to look back on flights.
- **A matching companion guide:** a step-by-step flow and checklist for the Fenix A320 in the same design. It's now in its third version and covers everything from cold-and-dark cockpit to shutdown, including approach variants and diagrams.

**The AI angle** (a fun segment for the hosts). The tour was designed, fact-checked and rebuilt with several AI assistants:

- One version was drafted with one model and reworked by another.
- For the later rounds, two AI models worked as a team with a strict "co-working protocol":
  - exactly one writer per file;
  - frozen file versions for reviews;
  - handoff notes like a real engineering team.
- The AI researched hundreds of airport sceneries against fixed rating thresholds, checked which airports are actually operating (and which airspace is closed), and wrote every briefing.
- Then the human pilot asked the sharp questions, like *"Is that really the latest version you're hosting?"* and *"What about the new Skiathos scenery?"*

## 12. Rapid-fire fun facts for the show
